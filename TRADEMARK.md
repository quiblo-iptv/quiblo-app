# The Quiblo name

**The code is free. The name is how people tell whose build they are running.**

Quiblo is licensed under the GPLv3, and that licence gives you the right to use, study, modify
and redistribute this software. Nothing on this page takes any of that away, and nothing on this
page can — a trademark policy governs a *name*, not a codebase.

What it is for is narrow and worth saying plainly: someone will eventually take this code, add a
bundled playlist, and ship it under a name close enough to ours that the difference does not
survive a screenshot. The fork is their right. The confusion is the part we are asking about.

**This is not legal advice and it was not drafted by a lawyer.**

## What the mark covers

The word **Quiblo**, and the application icon once there is a final one. Not the code, which the
GPLv3 covers; not the ideas; not the file formats.

## What you may do without asking anyone

Said first, and generously, because that is the point:

- **Fork it, modify it, redistribute it.** That is the licence, and the licence wins.
- **Say your project is derived from Quiblo, based on Quiblo, or a fork of Quiblo.** Accurate
  description of a relationship is not misuse of a name, and we would rather you said it.
- **Keep the name where it is historical** — in commit history, in attribution, in a changelog,
  in the licence headers. Rewriting history to erase it would be worse than leaving it.
- **Distribute unmodified builds under the name.** A packager who ships what we published,
  unchanged, is distributing Quiblo, and that is what the name is for.
- **Write about it, review it, teach with it, screenshot it.** No permission needed, and no
  approval sought.

## What we are asking you not to do

- **Ship a modified build under the same name and icon.** Change either the name or the icon —
  one is enough for a person to tell your build from ours. This is the whole of the request.
- **Use a name or icon close enough to be mistaken for ours.** "Quiblo Pro", "Quiblo+",
  "Quiblo TV" and a recoloured version of the icon are all the same problem: the person choosing
  a download cannot tell.
- **Imply that we endorse, maintain or support your build.** We cannot support code we did not
  write, and a user who believes otherwise will come to us with faults we cannot reproduce.
- **Ship a build that bundles content, under this name.** This one matters more than the rest
  and is the reason the page exists. Quiblo supplies no playlists, no channel lists and no way
  to find any — that is a
  [non-goal we wrote down before writing the app](docs/FREEZE.md), not an omission. A build that
  bundles content under our name attaches that content to a project that refused it, and the
  first people to draw that connection will not be reading a licence file.

## If we think something crosses the line

In order, and this is the order it would actually happen in:

1. **We ask.** Almost every case is somebody who did not think about it, and almost every case
   ends here. Renaming a fork is an afternoon.
2. **We say so publicly, without heat.** A short note in this repository saying which builds are
   ours. That is a statement of fact, and it costs a user nothing to check.
3. **We ask the distribution channel.** A store or an index has its own rules about impersonation
   and generally applies them faster than anyone else can.

**What we would not do**, stated so nobody has to guess: we are not going to threaten litigation
we have no intention of funding. A policy that threatens what nobody will pursue is worse than no
policy — it teaches people that the document is decoration.

> **Owner decision, still open:** whether the project would go beyond step 3, and in what
> circumstances. Until that is answered here in the owner's own words, treat the three steps
> above as the whole of it. See [`docs/STOPPERS.md`](docs/STOPPERS.md) §S8.

## Is the mark registered?

> **Owner decision, still open.** Assume **no** until this line says otherwise, and read the
> rest of this page as a request rather than a demand.

Two things are worth knowing while it is open. Unregistered rights in a name can exist through
use, and vary a great deal by country; and registration is a cost-and-jurisdiction decision that
belongs to whoever would pay for and defend it — not to this document. Saying "not registered"
plainly is better than letting a confident tone imply otherwise.

## If you are unsure

Ask, in an issue on this repository. A question costs us a minute; guessing wrong costs you a
rename.
