**The Work That Runs On Its Own of Quiblo — a catalogue that keeps itself, and the resume point
that kept being lost**

Everything in this round happens when nobody is looking at it. Two scheduled jobs bring the
catalogue and the popular lists up to date without waiting to be asked; a resume point is written
somewhere the screen going away cannot cancel; and a film's details stop sitting at the top of an
empty television screen.

**Created:** 2026-08-16, against `7533b34`.
**Ships as:** `1.0.0` work, `feat:` and `fix:`. A merge to `main` cuts a release; it is not merged
until that is asked for.

**Branch:** `feat/024-scheduled-work`, on top of `feat/023-for-you-shelves`.

---

## The defect that had three causes

**A viewer watches four minutes of a film, presses back, and is offered Play.** It was reported as
one bug and it is three, which is why it kept nearly going away.

**The read raced the write.** Both detail screens read the position once, from a `LifecycleResumeEffect`
that fires the instant the screen returns to the foreground — which is the same instant the player
above it is being disposed and is writing the position it just finished with. Nothing ordered the
two. Worse, having read once, neither screen ever asked again: the wrong answer stood until the
viewer left the screen and came back, which is the only workaround anybody found.

Both screens now collect a Room flow. **That does not order the read against the write either; it
removes the need to.** A write that lands a hundred milliseconds late still lands, and the button
follows it. This is the structural half of the fix and the reason it should stay fixed: there is no
ordering left to get wrong.

**The write was cancelled.** On the phone the player's ViewModel belongs to its navigation entry,
so `popBackStack()` clears that entry's store and cancels `viewModelScope` — the back press that
makes a position worth saving is the same event that cancels the coroutine saving it. The
`onCleared` fallback cannot cover it: androidx closes the scope *before* calling `onCleared`, so a
`viewModelScope.launch` there is a no-op by construction.

The write moves to an `ApplicationScope`. It is its own type rather than a bare `CoroutineScope`
because Koin resolves by type and does not type-check what it hands over, and a mis-wired scope
would be work quietly running in the wrong place.

**The write was rare.** A position reached the database at stop and at dispose, and both assume the
app gets to run afterwards. A process killed for memory, a television switched off at the wall, a
crash: each lost everything since the title was opened.

It is now written every ten seconds *of playback* — driven by the position crossing a boundary
rather than by a timer. A timer keeps running while a film is paused and while a viewer reads a
description, writing the same number over and over; watching the position writes exactly when there
is something new to say and stops on its own when nothing is moving.

## Decisions, and what they rule out

**WorkManager, not a foreground service.** It survives a reboot, respects doze, and is what the
system will actually let run. A foreground service for a four-day job would be a permanent
notification for work the viewer never asked to watch — and on a television, one nobody can see.

**`:feature:sync`, not `:core:sync`.** `FREEZE.md` §4.1 requires a desktop or webOS frontend to
consume `:core:*` untouched, and WorkManager is Android's scheduler. A `:core:` module naming it
would force the platform's scheduler on a platform that does not have one. The module carries no
Compose, so it uses the same convention plugin the core modules do.

**The scheduled sync merges; the one a person presses still replaces.** `replaceForSource` deletes
every row of a source and reinserts, giving each channel a new autogenerated id. That is tolerable
while somebody is watching it happen. Four days later, unattended, it destroys two things: the
arrival dates behind Recently Added — an M3U playlist carries none of its own, so the only date
there has ever been is when *we* first saw the title, and a rebuild would declare the whole
catalogue new every four days forever — and the row ids anything already holding one is pointing
at. So a surviving title keeps its id and its date, an arriving one is dated now, and a dropped one
is deleted, because a catalogue that only grows is not a catalogue.

**`KEEP`, not `UPDATE`.** The schedule is registered on every launch. `UPDATE` would restart the
interval each time, so a household that opens Quiblo every evening would never reach four days and
the sync would silently never run — for exactly the viewers who use the app most. `SyncSchedulerTest`
asserts this, because nothing at the call site says it.

**A network, and nothing else.** No charging requirement and no idle requirement: a television is
plugged in and never idle by the system's definition, and a phone that only synced while charging
would sync at night or not at all. Battery-not-low is absent for the same reason — these are two
HTTP requests, not a video encode.

**No key means success, not retry.** A worker that treated a missing metadata key as a failure
would be re-run with backoff forever on every installation that has never configured one, which is
the default. `AC-META-01` also says nothing here issues a request without a key.

**Forty hours replaces a week.** The old interval was argued partly on cost — two requests however
often the app is opened — and a scheduled check is what makes cost independent of that. The
repository still owns the guard, so the worker asking and a viewer arriving cannot between them
cost two fetches.

**A film's details are centred by a minimum height, not by a branch.** The column's minimum height
is the viewport's, so content shorter than the screen centres and content longer than it starts at
the top and scrolls exactly as before. One rule rather than a decision about how much text a
provider happened to send. The phone is unchanged: its portrait layout leads with a full-bleed
backdrop, which is meant to be at the top, and its landscape layout already fills its row.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Backing out of a film or an episode offers Resume, every time, with no flicker of Play.
2. Killing the app mid-playback loses at most ten seconds of position.
3. A live channel is never written to the history, however long it is left on.
4. A film with a short description sits centred on the television; a long one still scrolls.
5. The catalogue is re-read every four days without anybody asking.
6. That sync keeps ids, arrival dates, favourites and resume points, and removes titles the
   provider has dropped.
7. The popular lists are checked every forty hours, at a cost of at most two requests in that
   window, and not at all without a metadata key.
8. Scheduling on every launch does not postpone either job.

## Testing

Automated, all on the JVM:

- `PlayerResumeWriteTest` (new, 4) — the write lands **without the screen's own scope being
  advanced at all**, which is what fails when it is moved back to `viewModelScope`; ten-second
  boundaries write and the positions between them do not; live writes nothing; a position still at
  zero writes nothing.
- `MovieDetailResumeTest` (new, 3) — a position arriving *after* the screen has settled turns Play
  into Resume with nothing re-entering the screen; a position removed takes it away again; under
  ten seconds is still Play.
- `CatalogueMergeTest` (new, 7) — a surviving title keeps its id and its first-seen date; an
  arriving one is dated now; the provider's own date wins over both; a dropped one is removed; a
  favourite and a resume point survive; a first sync of an empty source inserts everything.
- `SyncSchedulerTest` (new, 3) — both jobs, their intervals, their constraints, and that scheduling
  twice does not restart the interval.
- `SyncWorkersTest` (new, 6) — the sync merges rather than replaces; one failed source is a retry
  and the others are still done; no sources is success; **no key is success and asks nothing**; a
  refusal is a retry; an answer is success.
- `PopularTitlesRepositoryTest` — the interval cases follow the new forty hours.

Gates: `detektAll`, `test`, `coverageAll` (`:core:data` at 74.7 against its floor of 70),
`licenceCheck` — five new Apache-2.0 AndroidX components, all WorkManager's — and `lint`. All green.

Manual: `docs/TESTING-REQUIRED.md` §A17, eight tickets. **A17.1 asks for five attempts on each app
rather than one**, because a race lost one time in five looks fixed if you try it twice. A17.4 to
A17.8 need a device left alone for days, which is the one thing a build cannot compress.

## Open, and owned by the author

- **Whether four days is right.** It is a guess about how often a provider adds things, made
  without a working account to measure. `docs/STOPPERS.md` S2.

  **Answered on 2026-09-05, and it was not right (`FEAT-031`).** Four days meant a title a
  provider had added could not be seen for most of a week. It is four hours now, and settable —
  the right number depends on one provider's habits and one panel's tolerance, neither of which
  is knowable from here.
- **What a sync should do when a provider has replaced every stable key at once.** Some panels
  rotate stream URLs, and a source whose keys all changed looks to the merge like a catalogue where
  everything was dropped and everything else arrived. The result is correct — the same titles, new
  ids, dated today — but Recently Added would be the whole catalogue for thirty days.
- **Whether the phone's film details should centre too.** They are unchanged here, deliberately.
- **Whether to merge.** The branch carries `feat:` commits, so a merge to `main` publishes a
  release.
